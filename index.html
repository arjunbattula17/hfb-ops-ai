Q4 — Fairness: who gets served, and by how much

How the AI decides fairness

Not largest-pantry-first, not closest-first, not a flat equal split. Two rules, applied in order:

  1. Per-item fair share: each expiring food item is split across the pantries that actually requested it, proportional to families served.
  2. Capacity-constrained max-min fairness: when total demand exceeds truck capacity, fulfillment % is equalized across pantries (water-filling) — pantries with small asks get 100% first, and any shortfall lands on whichever pantry has the largest remaining request, not the "biggest" pantry by definition.

This protects small/specialized pantries (e.g. Pantry C, bread-only) from being crowded out by large multi-category requesters (Pantry D), without ignoring D's real need.

Methodology & assumptions

Built on the Houston Food Bank case-study dataset (1 warehouse · 4 pantries · 3 trucks · 5 inventory items) — architecture scales to the full network (5 warehouses · 50 pantries · 20 trucks). All figures computed client-side, live, from the data in data.js.
